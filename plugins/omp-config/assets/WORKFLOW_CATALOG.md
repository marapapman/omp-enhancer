# OMP Enhancer Workflow Catalog v46

Advisory reference. Main orchestrates freely through ANALYZE -> EXECUTE -> REVIEW.

## `writing`

- When: Prose and copy in any language (English, Chinese) or format (LaTeX, Markdown, Beamer, Word, PowerPoint/PPTX): the dedicated text tools writer/zh-writer author every wording surface, while Main/task handle code, evidence, structure, layout, conversion, and validation.
- Skills: `writing-review`, `format-humanizer`, `plain-chinese-writing`, `zh-research-achievement-writing`, `zh-format-humanizer`, `zh-writing-review`, `zh-writing-polish`, `writing-markdown-helper`, `zh-writing-markdown-helper`, `format-markdown2latex`, `format-latex2markdown`, `format-template-latex`, `latex-beamer-slides`, `beamer-to-powerpoint`, `slides-storyline`, `storyline-workbench`, `docx`
- Agents (host runtime adapter labels): `writer`, `zh-writer`, `checker`, `zh-checker`, `task`
- Flow:
  1. Identify target language (zh/en) and format (plain/LaTeX/Markdown/Beamer/Word/PowerPoint/PPTX).
  2. Load matching language and format skills.
  3. For Chinese prose, prepare the genre reference before drafting by picking the card that matches the task purpose (technical explanation, teaching, academic body text, research-achievement summary, everyday communication, or slide body 幻灯片正文) from skill://plain-chinese-writing/references/style-profiles.md and handing it to the zh-writer assignment together with the target readers, the selectable excerpt and its source, and the separately marked fact material, because the baseline itself is universal across Chinese writing while the research-narrative register activates only for the two research cards (academic body text 学术正文 and research-achievement summary 科研成果凝练), and the card supplies content organization and the excerpt; reading that reference is genre material retrieval that does not activate the plain-chinese-writing Skill, so skills=none stays valid for the writer.
  4. The substantive-text sequence starts with a proposal from the language-matched dedicated text tool, writer (English) or zh-writer (Chinese), covering drafting, logical/semantic revision, translation, polishing, titles, labels, captions, notes, and UI copy; before drafting a new outline or revising an existing draft, Main builds a chunk list in source order, with Chinese chunks at most 2,000 non-whitespace characters, English chunks at most 1,200 words, and slide chunks at most 6 pages, splitting by section, consecutive paragraphs, or consecutive pages and never padding a chunk to reach the limit, keeping a paragraph, a table with its scope note, a formula with its definition, and code with its needed explanation intact, and letting an indivisible unit over the limit become its own chunk with the exception recorded and no content truncated; every chunk goes to a new writer/zh-writer sub-task that never continues the previous chunk, never resumes its session, and never receives the whole document or another chunk's round trips, and this chunk independence never requires a different model; at most four such sub-tasks run at once, and a host with less capacity batches them while still creating a new context per chunk. Each draft follows the target body language's built-in reference style carried by that matching text tool; mixed-language content uses separate writer and zh-writer text-tool calls by segment; Main preserves semantic anchors and integrates the returned proposal verbatim.
  5. Checker/zh-checker review, when used for early feedback, reports logic/evidence issues and sentences that add no new fact, a necessary qualification, or a key logical relation without rewriting prose; it is not the final review — the scan step below runs before the final checker review and delivery — and any substantive repair returns to the same language-matched text tool. Main integrates exact writer output, while Main/task/checker never rewrite prose; code, evidence collection, structural reordering, layout, conversion, validation, and other non-text actions may remain with Main/task.
  6. If the writer text tool cannot safely handle substantive English prose (or zh-writer Chinese prose), record that inability as a limitation rather than silently drafting through another role.
  7. For Word output, apply the approved writer/zh-writer content through officecli (docx skill) and verify with officecli validate and a rendered view; task may perform only structural, layout, conversion, and validation operations and must not change wording.
  8. For a new article or a new slide deck, build and confirm the story as a tree in the storyline-workbench site before any prose; the site freezes the confirmed tree and its open comments into an immutable round snapshot, the language-matched writer/zh-writer writes from that snapshot, and an already confirmed story can be loaded into the site for another round.
  9. For new Beamer decks, the writer/zh-writer text tool produces the Markdown content plan whose titles avoid the "XX：XX" label+colon+label pattern and read as coherent prose in deck order; confirm it with the user, let a separate task reconcile slide order and structure, then have the task convert the unchanged text-tool output into Beamer and begin layout.
  10. After either a new or existing Beamer deck reaches its final validated Beamer visual revision, branch optionally to PPTX output when requested: use the fixed external `beamer2pptx` Skill/repository at https://github.com/xdmlxdml/beamer2pptx/tree/main/beamer2pptx with the final validated PDF and corresponding `.tex`, macro, and font sources when available; a producing task performs only PPTX conversion, binding, rendering, and bounded fixes, then one independent read-only reviewer checks the current PPTX renders. The producing task may apply at most one bounded fix, rerender fresh evidence, and the same reviewer confirms only those findings once. Keep this branch advisory with no automatic loop or hard gate; preserve visible content, formulas, slide order, and Markdown/Beamer sources. A content or layout finding is resolved in place at the stage where it is found; it is not propagated back to the Markdown content plan; a prose wording change is still produced by the language-matched writer/zh-writer text tool and applied in place only as that verbatim proposal; task authors no wording.
  11. For substantial de-AI work, apply the format-humanizer/zh-format-humanizer rules one at a time before checking: a scan task inventories the tell families present, then one writer/zh-writer text-tool pass per found family runs over bounded chunks, with the non-overlapping chunks of that family dispatched as separate fresh writer/zh-writer sub-tasks that may run in parallel; the family passes run strictly sequentially, and the next family starts only after every authorized chunk of the current family is complete and its semantic-anchor check verified by Main; all rewriting stays with the text tools and no pass mixes families.
  12. After every completed writing task, once the language-matched text tool has run its sentence-level information-gain self-check on its own final text, call writing_ai_scan with detector=ensemble and allowNetwork=true on that text before checker review and delivery; when it reports a tell family, route one language-matched writer/zh-writer repair pass per family and rerun the scan on the revised text. Record the last scan result and its AIGC+Jev decision with the delivery; the scan is advisory evidence, not a quality verdict, and neither the authorship score nor the local metrics can replace the sentence-level information-gain review.
  13. Check via checker/zh-checker for substantial work to report logic, semantic, evidence, and clarity issues, including sentences that add no new fact, a necessary qualification, or a key logical relation, all read-only and without rewriting prose; after chunked integration, run a read-only consistency check covering each chunk's fidelity and style against its own source, every adjacent pair's referent and continuation, document-wide terminology and subject use, repeated closers and page templateization, and style coverage across the opening, middle, and closing chunks; that check may be split across chunks so no single checker receives the whole document, each problem is located to a specific chunk, and every wording repair still goes to a new writer/zh-writer sub-task with no final whole-document polish pass; Main checks orchestration, preservation, and consistency only and never authors or edits text.
  14. Deliver with preservation and consistency verification.

## `research`

- When: Source-backed research, web synthesis, comparison, recommendation, fact-checking, or claim-by-claim verdict.
- Skills: `fact-checking`, `claim-extraction`, `source-evaluation`, `citation-authenticity`
- Agents (host runtime adapter labels): `fact-researcher-a`, `fact-researcher-b`, `fact-researcher-c`, `fact-challenger`, `fact-planner`, `scout`, `writer`, `zh-writer`
- Flow:
  1. Decompose into checkable claims or research questions.
  2. Collect evidence from primary sources; corroborate with multiple sources.
  3. Cross-check evidence lanes for agreement, conflicts, and staleness.
  4. Route all user-facing research text through the language-matched dedicated text tool before synthesis: English goes to writer, Chinese goes to zh-writer, and mixed-language output uses separate writer and zh-writer text-tool calls.
  5. The writer/zh-writer text tool drafts the source-linked synthesis with confidence levels; Main integrates the returned text verbatim.
  6. Review verdicts for overclaiming; report limitations without drafting or rewriting prose.

## `visual`

- When: Diagrams (draw.io), UI/UX design, static visual artifacts, or rendered figure review.
- Skills: `drawio-skill`, `assetseeker`, `frontend-design`, `canvas-design`, `format-humanizer`, `zh-format-humanizer`
- Agents (host runtime adapter labels): `task`, `writer`, `zh-writer`
- Flow:
  1. Clarify the diagram type, target language, information claims, real nodes and directed relations (including conditions and grouping meaning), output paths, physical width and the destination rectangle's aspect ratio (4:3 or 16:9 for PPT, page-width or half-page for A4/US letter), the at most 3 key messages the figure must foreground, and any names, math symbols, or terms that must not change. Before drawing, route every user-facing English label, caption, narrative, and UI copy to the dedicated text tool writer and every Chinese equivalent to the dedicated text tool zh-writer (mixed-language output uses separate writer and zh-writer text-tool calls); task may perform only structural mapping, drawing, layout, export, and evidence-based review. For diagram-only branches, use drawio-skill (drawio@365-skills) plus the one authoring reference for the chosen method. Asset prep is a default planning step: Main names the hero node or path, the lead decorative hue, and an icon for every concrete component node, then, unless the user asked for vector-only or text-only output, pre-generates one coherent house-style icon family with the native generate_image tool (style board as input), adds Iconify glyphs via assetseeker for standard technical symbols, assetseeker stock for photographic content, and bl image via bash for Chinese-text or photoreal nodes, recording provenance before drawing. For a new figure Main may also generate one composition thumbnail as a private layout reference; its text is ignored and it is never delivered.
  2. After writer/zh-writer returns the copy, task derives a stable node/edge ID list from the source material, maps the text verbatim, then draws the diagram once with drawio-skill and exports: a preview PNG (no -e, longest side <= 2000), a cropped single-page vector PDF, and a final PNG (-e -s 2) repaired with the skill's repair_png.py. Ask the user only when the diagram meaning or physical size changes.
  3. Exactly one independent read-only review of the actual exports: Main when it has image input, otherwise one image-capable task that did not author the diagram (a text-only task model cannot read the exports and is never assigned the review). The reviewer checks node/edge semantics, edges crossing unrelated boxes, overlapping or collinear edges, arrow attribution, arrowhead size against the visible shaft (arrowheads small on a shaft of at least 3× their length), text clipping, font size at physical width, alignment, grouping margins, reading direction, gray-scale distinguishability, and the color quota (white/black base, no gray or pale fills, small saturated accent blocks from the fixed palette (red #D5171A failure/risk, teal #037870 success/verification, blue #1668B5 and amber #E08A1E decorative-only), no AI rainbow per-element or per-category color assignment); it also checks the aesthetic recipe — a readable hero hierarchy, content filling about 65–85% of the frame, palette ink at roughly 5–12% with an accent thread along the key message, one consistent icon family, badges anchored to their node, and the stroke and type tiers — and enforces the scientific-figure limits — node count within 5-8, at most 3 key messages dominating the canvas, labels terse, vivid icons instead of bare text boxes, and the destination frame ratio preserved in the exports; findings name node/edge IDs, location, and one executable local fix.
  4. The task applies at most one local structure/layout fix round for supported findings (keeping other node/edge IDs and positions), re-exports the same source set, and the same reviewer confirms against the fresh exports that recorded findings are resolved and no new regression was introduced; any text finding returns to writer/zh-writer instead of being edited by task or Main. This confirmation is not a second review pass or a new design round. Remaining findings are reported as limitations.
  5. Deliver the .drawio source plus the exports the task actually needs (default for paper/report: .drawio + cropped vector PDF + clean PNG); Main retains setup authorization and final acceptance only.
